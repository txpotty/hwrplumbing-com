    Electric Thermostat Service in Mitchell

    Each element on an electric tank has its own thermostat, and the upper one carries a reset button for the high-limit switch. A tripped reset is a symptom. We find out why it tripped, which usually comes down to a shorted element or a thermostat stuck closed, before we press it back in. Power stays off at the breaker the whole time.

    Expansion Tank Service in Mitchell, SD

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. We test the air side with a tire gauge. The precharge needs to match static pressure in the house. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Mitchell, SD

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. A different rod usually solves it. We replace it with an aluminum-zinc rod or a powered anode, then chlorinate the tank to kill the bacteria living in it. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Mitchell, SD

    The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. Mineral scale builds up quickly in water heaters, faucet aerators and fixtures here, so a water softener is a common upgrade for homeowners. We loosen the hex head with a breaker bar and an impact gun, pull the rod and look at how much core wire is showing. Six inches of bare wire means replace it.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Mitchell

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. The new valve must match the tank's BTU or kilowatt rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. Never plug or cap that pipe.

    Sediment Flushing in Mitchell, SD

    Sediment is mostly calcium carbonate and sand, and it settles in a layer that can reach several inches on a neglected 50-gallon tank. That layer insulates water from the heat. Our method is short bursts of line pressure that churn up the bottom. The drain hose runs to a floor drain or outside, never onto a lawn where hot water can burn the grass.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Mitchell

    Street pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house, and plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve above that number. A gauge on a hose bib tells us the static pressure. Then we lift the relief valve lever. If the valve opens and reseats cleanly and the expansion tank holds its charge, the tank is safe from pressure spikes.
