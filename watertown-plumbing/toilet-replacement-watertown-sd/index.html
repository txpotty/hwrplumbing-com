    Toilet Flange Repair During Replacement in Watertown

    Pulling the old toilet is the only chance to see the flange, and a cracked plastic ring or a rusted cast iron flange will ruin a new install within a year. We check it every time. For a cracked flange, a stainless repair ring is often enough, while a flange that sits below the finished floor gets a spacer or extender so the seal compresses properly.

    Commercial Flush Valve Upgrades in Watertown

    Manual flush valves on older commercial toilets leak at the handle and stick open, and swapping to a sensor valve during a replacement improves hygiene without changing the rough-in.     Replacing Pressure-Assisted Toilets in Watertown, SD

    Pressure-assisted toilets use a sealed vessel in the tank that stores air under line pressure and pushes water through the bowl hard, which helps in houses with long, low-slope drain runs. They are noticeably louder than gravity toilets. Replacement means matching the vessel brand for parts later, and we check that house pressure is in the range the unit needs.

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Watertown, SD

    Toilets made before 1994 often use 3.5 gallons a flush, and some from the 1970s use five or more.     ADA and Commercial Toilet Replacement in Watertown, SD

    Water-Saving Toilet Upgrades in Watertown, SD

    Older toilets are one of the largest indoor water users in a house, and replacing a 3.5 gallon model with a 1.28 gallon one cuts toilet water use by more than half.     Toilet Rough-In Adjustments in Watertown, SD

Take the measurement from the wall to the closet bolts, not from the baseboard trim. If the new toilet's rough-in does not match, it either sits too far out or will not fit. Offset flanges can fix a small mismatch.
