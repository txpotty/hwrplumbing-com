    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Belle Fourche

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. We pipe the air intake the way the manufacturer and the code both require. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. We also set the unit level within a quarter inch, since a tilted trap short-circuits the baffles and loses capacity.

    Hydromechanical Grease Trap Installation in Belle Fourche

    Flow rating is the number that matters on a hydromechanical trap, and it has to match the fastest-draining fixture combination in your kitchen. Put a 50 GPM unit on a 75 GPM load and grease goes out the back at every rush. We do the math on paper. The sink volume and drain-down time give the GPM number, and we show you the calculation.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Belle Fourche

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. Most of the difference is labor, mainly digging and concrete repair. Permits add a little. We quote in writing after a site visit, with the trap, the labor and the permit fees listed as separate lines.

    Size comes from flow, and flow comes from your fixtures. We measure each connected fixture and calculate the gallons per minute they can send at once. Gravity tanks use volume instead. For those, the local pretreatment program often has its own formula based on seats or fixture units, and we use theirs.

    Almost always, yes. You will usually need a plumbing permit plus a sign-off from the sewer authority's pretreatment program. Our office handles the permit paperwork and books the inspections. Expect an inspector on site.

    Plan on a permit. Both the building department and the sewer authority usually have a say. You get copies of every approval for your files. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    Zurn and Watts make up a lot of our indoor installs, with Schier and Endura when a plastic body makes more sense. Outdoor interceptors are usually precast concrete, or polyethylene where a crane cannot reach. Any certified brand works. What we will not install is an uncertified import trap, because inspectors reject them and the parts disappear after a year.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. The trade usually calls the small indoor unit a trap and the big outdoor tank an interceptor. Codes vary on the wording. Many codes say hydromechanical interceptor and gravity interceptor instead, and we match the local wording on the permit.

    Two warranties apply here. Manufacturer coverage on the unit varies by brand and material, and plastic bodies usually get the longer terms. We cover our workmanship, meaning leaks at our joints and settling of a tank we set, and we give you the terms in writing before we start.

    From first call to final inspection, most indoor jobs take one to two weeks, and most of that is waiting on the permit and the part. The install itself is often a single day. Outdoor jobs run longer. Gravity interceptors can take three to six weeks end to end because of plan review and tank delivery.
