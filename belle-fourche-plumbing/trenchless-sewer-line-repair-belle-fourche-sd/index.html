    Cured-In-Place Pipe Relining in Belle Fourche, SD

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. The crew saturates a felt tube with two-part epoxy on a roller table at the truck, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Cure time runs a few hours. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.

    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Replacement in Belle Fourche

    Orangeburg is bituminous fiber pipe that was laid in houses mostly from the late 1940s into the early 1970s.     Causes of Sewer Line Damage in Belle Fourche, SD

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints pull apart or crack. Soils in South Dakota range from glacial deposits in the east to clay rich ground farther west that swells when wet and shrinks when dry. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Then age finishes the job. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under the weight of soil and traffic.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Lining in Belle Fourche, SD

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. That is called channel rot. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, that beats jackhammering.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Prep and Lining in Belle Fourche

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line to remove roots and grease, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. A poor prep job means a wrinkled liner. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Root Intrusion Fixes in Belle Fourche, SD

    Roots enter at the joints, where clay bells or cast iron hubs have a small gap or a failed gasket. Once in, they fill the pipe. We cut them out with a mechanical root cutter or a hydro jet, then line the pipe so those joints are sealed for good and the line stops needing a cleaning every few months. The roots cannot get back in.
