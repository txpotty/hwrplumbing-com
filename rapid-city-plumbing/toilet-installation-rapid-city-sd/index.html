    Toilet Installation Jobs We Handle in Rapid City, SD

    Pressure-Assisted Toilet Installation in Rapid City

    Pressure-assisted toilets have a sealed vessel inside the tank that uses line pressure to push the flush. Below about 20 psi, they flush poorly. Before suggesting one, we put a gauge on a hose bib, and we mention the noise, because the flush carries through the whole house. They rarely clog.

    Two-Piece Toilet Installation in Rapid City

    Two-piece toilets come with the tank and bowl separate, joined by a rubber gasket and two or three bolts. We assemble them on the floor before setting. Even tightening, alternating sides, matters more than force, since uneven pressure either cracks the china or leaves one bolt weeping. It should not wobble.

    Urgent Toilet Replacement in Rapid City

    A cracked tank or a bowl leaking at the base cannot wait for next week, especially in a house with one bathroom. We keep common two-piece toilets on the truck. In many cases we can pull the broken one and set a replacement in the same visit, with a 12 inch rough-in model in round or elongated. Close the stop until then.

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Rapid City, SD

    High-efficiency toilets flush on 1.28 gallons or less, against 3.5 gallons or more for toilets from before the early 1990s. Check for the WaterSense label. A MaP rating of 800 grams or better is what we look for, since a weak low-flow toilet that needs double flushing saves no water at all. Dual-flush models add a lighter half flush.

    New Construction Toilet Installation in Rapid City

    On new builds we set toilets after the finish floor is down, never before. A flange belongs flush with or just above finished flooring. An early set means the tile goes around the flange, leaving it low, and that calls for an extender ring later. Each bowl gets a fresh seal.

    Smart Toilet Installation in Rapid City, SD

    Smart toilets with heated seats and built-in bidets need two things the old toilet did not: power and a dedicated water connection. Most need a GFCI outlet close enough for the cord. We run the water side and mount the unit, and if a new outlet is needed we bring in a licensed electrician rather than splicing anything ourselves. Then we test every button.

    Toilet Flange and Wax Ring Replacement in Rapid City, SD

    The closet flange is the ring that ties the toilet to the drain, and it is the weak point on most old installs. On cast iron, the bolt slots rust out first. PVC and ABS flanges crack when someone overtightens the bolts, and a repair ring screwed over the top is often all it takes to restore a solid anchor. Seal goes on last.

    Bathroom Remodel Toilet Installation in Rapid City, SD

    New tile over old vinyl can raise a bathroom floor by half an inch or more. Suddenly the old flange is below the floor. We bring the flange up with a stacking extender or replace it outright, and if you are moving the toilet on a slab, that means cutting concrete to reroute the drain and patching it afterward. Moving it on a wood floor is easier.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Rapid City

    Most homeowners in Rapid City, SD pay $200 to $500 to have a toilet installed, fixture not included. Removing a stubborn old unit adds time. Bolts rusted solid have to be cut with a hacksaw or oscillating tool, and a cast iron flange that breaks during removal needs a repair ring, each of which adds labor.

    Plan on one to three hours for a straight swap. Most of that is prep, not setting the bowl. Add a new flange or a subfloor patch and it can run to half a day, which we will flag as soon as we see it.

    Just about any brand you can buy at a supply house or home center. TOTO and American Standard are the ones we set most often. Landlords like Gerber and Mansfield for cheap, easy parts, and a quick call is usually enough for us to point you to a model. Bring us the model number.

    About two hours for most floor-mount toilets. Wall-mounted and smart toilets take longer. Wall-mounted units usually need two visits, carrier first and bowl after the finish wall, and a smart toilet can hinge on the electrician.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Most homes measure 12 inches. You will also see 10 and 14 inch rough-ins, and buying the wrong one means the toilet will not fit or sits well off the wall. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. A simple swap usually goes without. The permit covers drain sizing and venting, and the inspector wants to see both before the floor or wall is closed, so we schedule the inspection around your remodel timeline.

    Two warranties apply. The toilet is covered by its maker. Parts usually get one to five years and the china often far longer, and our guarantee picks up any leak caused by the install.

    Watch the base and the tank. Water that reappears on the floor after you wipe it up points to a cracked bowl or a failed seal. A seal can be replaced for far less than a new toilet, so we check that first, but a crack in the china means replacement, since there is no lasting repair for cracked porcelain.
