    Copper Service Line Installation in Rapid City, SD

    Copper is still the choice for some homeowners. It has a long track record. We flare or use brass compression fittings at the ends, keep the run in one piece between them, and pad it with sand to keep stones off. Where it goes through a concrete wall, it gets a plastic sleeve and a seal to keep water and bugs out.

    PEX Service Lines for Long Runs in Rapid City

    We buy PEX in long coils. That makes it the easy pick when the meter sits out at the road and the house is set far back, because one coil covers the whole run without a joint. Fewer joints underground means fewer places a leak can start, and it is the joints that usually fail first.

    Water Line Upgrades and Code Compliance in Rapid City, SD

    A lot of older houses were fed with a 1/2-inch or 3/4-inch line that was fine for one bathroom. Add a second bath and a dishwasher and it falls short. When we replace the line we size it to your fixture count, which often means going to 1-inch, and bring the shutoff, depth and backflow pieces up to what code asks for today. Better pressure upstairs.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Rapid City, SD

    Pipe pulling works like this. We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable or bursting head goes through the old pipe and drags new HDPE or PEX back through the same path, splitting the old pipe if it is brittle, so a driveway, a row of shrubs or a concrete walk can stay where it is.

    Polybutylene Water Line Replacement in Rapid City, SD

    Polybutylene is the gray or blue plastic pipe installed from roughly the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorine in the water breaks it down from the inside. The failures tend to be sudden splits rather than slow drips, and some home insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Worth replacing.

    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Rapid City

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. The zinc coating wears off and the steel rusts from the inside. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, which is why pressure drops and the water runs orange after a shutoff, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    PEX Water Line Installation in Rapid City, SD

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Frozen pipes are a real and regular risk in South Dakota, with deep frost each winter that can reach water service lines buried at shallow depth. A frozen PEX line often survives where copper would crack. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and we keep it out of direct sun at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

    Burst Main Line Response in Rapid City, SD

    Burst lines do damage fast. We get the water off, dig to the break, and decide on site whether a clamp or a short section of new pipe will hold until a full replacement can be scheduled, or whether the pipe crumbles when we touch it. You will not be without water overnight if we can help it.
