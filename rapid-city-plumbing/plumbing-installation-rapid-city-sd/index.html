    Gas Line Installation in Rapid City, SD

    A new range or an outdoor kitchen all start with the same math: total BTU load and the length of the run. We size the pipe from that, pick black iron or CSST, and trench where the line goes underground. Each joint is checked with leak solution. Then the whole line holds pressure on a gauge before the inspector sees it.

    PEX Pipe Installation in Rapid City, SD

    PEX bends around corners, shrugs off corrosion and needs far fewer fittings than copper. On a whole-house repipe that means fewer joints hidden behind drywall and fewer places for a slow leak to start rotting a stud. It can also stretch when water freezes. Frozen pipes are a real and regular risk in South Dakota, with deep frost each winter that can reach water service lines buried at shallow depth. Stretch or not, we still insulate runs in exterior walls and crawl spaces.

    Gas Line Installation for New Appliances in Rapid City, SD

    Water heaters, ranges, dryers, fireplaces and pool heaters all pull from the same gas service. Each branch is sized from the total BTU load and the run length so the last appliance on the line is not starved. New lines get pressure tested, always. Bigger loads sometimes need a meter upgrade, and we set that up with the gas utility.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Rapid City, SD

    Electric tankless heaters pull serious amperage, often 120 amps or more for a whole-house unit.     Water Line Installation and Replacement in Rapid City

    Corroded galvanized and brittle polybutylene are the two pipes we replace most.     Backflow Preventer Installation in Rapid City

    Irrigation systems and fire sprinkler lines are the usual reasons a home needs a backflow preventer. It keeps fertilizer-laced lawn water from being pulled back into the drinking supply when a main break or hydrant flush drops pressure. Type matters.     Sump Pump and Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Rapid City
