    Jet Pump System Repair in Sturgis, SD

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Air leaks on the suction side cause most failures. HWR Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and confirms it builds pressure. A jet pump that runs nonstop without reaching pressure has almost always lost prime or sucked air through a cracked fitting.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Sturgis

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. A waterlogged tank or a ruptured bladder causes most of it. We check the precharge with the tank drained. It should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure, so a 40/60 switch wants roughly 38 psi in the tank.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Sturgis, SD

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Summer thunderstorms can drop a lot of rain in a short time, pushing water back through floor drains when sewer lines are overloaded. Surges from lightning are the usual cause of control box failures. After testing the capacitor, relay and overload, we replace the bad part and suggest a surge arrestor if the box lacks one. Arrestors are cheap.
