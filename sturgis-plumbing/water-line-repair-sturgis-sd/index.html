    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Sturgis

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. Then the new pipe gets pulled through underground. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, and most jobs wrap up in a single day.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Sturgis

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Newer construction relies mostly on PEX supply lines and plastic drains, which handle freeze and thaw cycles better than rigid pipe. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. You get a price on every option.

    Water Line Repair at the Meter in Sturgis, SD

    The meter box is where a lot of leaks start. Compression fittings there work loose over time.     Commercial Water Line Repair in Sturgis, SD

    Water Pressure Restoration in Sturgis

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. First step is a pressure gauge on the outside spigot. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the house shutoff and the meter-side valve for a partly closed gate, and look at the pipe itself. Galvanized pipe can lose half its opening to rust scale. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Sturgis, SD

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Many rural homes in South Dakota draw from private wells or rural water systems, and a water test is the best way to know what comes out of the tap. Rust and scale close the pipe from the inside. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Most homes are done in a day.

Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. You see the cut-out section yourself.
