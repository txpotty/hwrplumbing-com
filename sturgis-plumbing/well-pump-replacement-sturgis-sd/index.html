    Well Pump Brands We Install in Sturgis, SD

    Goulds, Grundfos, Franklin Electric and Sta-Rite cover nearly every well pump we install. We pick by the well, not the logo. Parts for Goulds and Franklin are easy to get, which matters when a control box dies on a weekend.

    Constant Pressure Pump Systems in Sturgis

    Franklin SubDrive and Grundfos SQE are the two constant pressure systems we install most. Both use a small 2 to 20 gallon tank instead of a big one, since the drive keeps the pump running at a matched speed. The soft start is easier on the motor. The upfront cost is higher.

    Pump and Pressure Tank Matching in Sturgis

    Pump and tank work as a pair. An undersized or waterlogged tank forces dozens of extra starts a day, and each start puts heat into the motor windings. We check it on every pump job.

    Pulling a Submersible Pump in Sturgis

    Pulling a submersible means lifting the pump and every length of drop pipe out through the pitless adapter, sometimes hundreds of feet of it. On the way up, we look at the torque arrestor and the splices. Figure on one day.

    Agricultural and Ranch Well Pumps in Sturgis

    Irrigation and stock wells often run 3 to 10 HP pumps, sometimes on three-phase power with a motor starter and overload protection at the panel. We replace those pumps and the starters that protect them. Timing matters on a ranch.

    Well Pump Sizing in Sturgis

    Sizing starts with the well log. We get depth and static level from the well log or a sounder, then a draw-down test shows what the well can really produce in gallons per minute. Pumps bigger than the well can supply run dry.

    Grundfos Well Pump Replacement in Sturgis, SD

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have a built-in soft start and dry-run protection, and they run on two wires with no control box at the surface. They cost more up front. Add the CU 301 controller to an SQE and you get constant pressure.

    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Sturgis

    Sand kills submersibles early. Grit in a well chews up impellers fast, so we look for it on the old pump and may set the new one higher or add a sand separator. Pump setting depth matters.
