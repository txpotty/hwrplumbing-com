    Appliance Connector and Shutoff Repair in Harrisburg

    Old flexible connectors, especially uncoated brass ones, crack and leak with age. We replace them with new coated stainless connectors sized for the appliance and add an accessible shutoff valve if the old one is missing or seized. This is a quick fix. Ranges, dryers, water heaters and furnaces all need proper connections, and most also need a sediment trap ahead of the appliance gas valve.

    Buried Yard Line Repair and Replacement in Harrisburg, SD

    Many older homes have steel gas lines running from the meter to the house or out to a pool heater. The wrap fails eventually. Once the coating cracks, the pipe starts to corrode, and wet soil from sprinklers or a low spot in the yard speeds that up a great deal. Utilities get marked first. The new line is polyethylene with tracer wire, or coated steel where it has to be exposed, and we backfill and clean up the yard when the test passes.

    Corroded Gas Pipe Replacement in Harrisburg

    Buried steel gas line corrodes once its coating cracks, and moisture, roots and ground movement speed it up. Soils in South Dakota range from glacial deposits in the east to clay rich ground farther west that swells when wet and shrinks when dry. We replace corroded sections with polyethylene pipe laid with tracer wire, using an approved transition riser where the line comes up out of the ground at the house. Exposed sections get coated steel. We test before backfilling so any problem joint is fixed while the trench is still open.

    Natural Gas Leak Detection and Repair in Harrisburg

    Finding a gas leak starts at the meter. With every appliance off, a moving test dial on the meter tells us gas is escaping somewhere, and then we isolate branches to narrow it down. An electronic detector does the rest. A spray of soap solution on the fitting shows bubbles at the leak, and we fix that joint or section and retest the whole system.

    Propane Gas Line Service in Harrisburg, SD

    Propane systems run at different pressures than natural gas and use a two-stage regulator setup between the tank and the house. Regulators fail too. We repair buried and above-ground propane lines, replace leaking regulators and fittings and check that each appliance has been converted with the correct orifices for propane. A natural gas burner running on propane will burn rich and soot up. We test the system the same way we test natural gas piping.
