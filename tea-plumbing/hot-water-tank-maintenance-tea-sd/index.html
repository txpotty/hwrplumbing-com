    Relief Valve Testing in Tea

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. We lift the test lever and watch it reseat. A valve that drips afterward gets replaced. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.

    Yearly Maintenance Plans in Tea, SD

    A maintenance plan puts your tank on the calendar so nobody has to remember. We call you when it is due. Plans cover one or two visits a year at a fixed rate, with priority scheduling if the heater fails between visits and a discount on parts like anodes and drain valves.     Attic and Garage Tank Checkups in Tea, SD

    Garage and attic tanks live through bigger temperature swings than closet units, which wears out plastic drain valves and wiring insulation faster. A pan under an attic tank is not optional. We check that the pan drain line runs to a safe spot and is not cracked or plugged, because one slow leak over a bedroom can bring down a ceiling. Gas tanks in garages need their ignition source at least 18 inches off the floor unless they are FVIR models.

    Emergency Tank Troubleshooting in Tea

    No hot water at all is usually a pilot, a gas valve, a tripped high-limit or a dead element. Most of those are quick fixes.     T&P Valve Replacement in Tea

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Tea

    Most flush and inspection visits in Tea land between $100 and $250, depending on tank size and how hard it is to reach. Anode replacement adds to that. You hear the flat price before we schedule.

    Once a year is the baseline almost everywhere. On hard water, go every six months. A softened, lightly used tank may go longer, with a yearly anode check.

    Water is trapped under a layer of sediment and boiling in small bursts as the burner fires. It is a sign to flush. A flush usually quiets it, though scale that has baked hard for years may not come out completely, and in that case the noise is a sign the tank is aging.

    Many manufacturers write regular maintenance into the warranty terms and can deny a claim for sediment damage. Read the fine print on yours. Our dated invoices give you that proof each year.

    Water chemistry decides how fast the anode gets eaten. Softened water is the hardest on rods. Sodium from a softener makes the water more conductive, so a magnesium rod can be gone in two or three years instead of five, which is why we check it every visit.

    It is a long rod of magnesium or aluminum screwed into the top of the tank. It corrodes so the tank does not. Once it is used up, the water starts attacking the glass lining and the steel behind it, and a tank with a spent anode can rust through in a few years.

    Keeping service records is the best protection you have. Manufacturers can deny sediment claims. Major brands mention maintenance, and adjusters ask about it.

    Our techs work on gas and electric tanks from every major maker, plus the store brands built in the same plants. Age of the tank does not matter. Some tanks we see from the 1990s still have the original anode.
