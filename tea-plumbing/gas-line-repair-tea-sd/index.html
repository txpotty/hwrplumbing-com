    Pool Heater, Fire Pit and Grill Lines in Tea, SD

    Backyard gas lines for pool heaters, fire pits and built-in grills face sun, weather and irrigation water that shorten their life. Repairs and new runs are tested, buried at code depth and marked with tracer wire.

    After-Hours Gas Line Repair in Tea

    Gas problems do not wait for business hours, and losing heat or hot water overnight is a real problem. We answer late calls. Once everyone is out and the gas company has secured things, we send a plumber to find the leak, fix it and test the system. We coordinate with the utility on the meter.

    Gas Appliance Connector Repair in Tea, SD

    Weak flames or a smell near one appliance often point to the connector or shutoff behind it. Uncoated brass connectors from decades ago crack at the flare fittings and should be replaced on sight. New coated connectors go in. We also add a reachable shutoff and a drip leg where needed, and confirm the new connector is rated for that appliance's BTU input.

    Gas Line Extensions and Relocations in Tea

    Adding a gas fireplace, moving a laundry room or switching a range from electric to gas usually means extending the gas line. Sizing comes first. Our plumbers total the BTU load, measure the run and size the pipe so the last appliance on the line still gets full pressure at peak use. Then we route it, tie it in and test it.

    Gas Leak Repair at Fittings and Valves in Tea

    Most residential gas leaks are at a threaded joint, a valve stem or a flexible connector, not in the middle of a pipe. That keeps repairs small. Leaking joints get taken apart, cleaned, resealed with gas-rated compound and tightened, and damaged fittings or valves get replaced. We soap-test the repaired joint and pressure test the system before leaving.

    Corroded Gas Pipe Replacement in Tea

    Buried steel gas line corrodes once its coating cracks, and moisture, roots and ground movement speed it up. Basements are common in South Dakota homes, which puts water heaters, softeners and main drains below grade where sump systems often play a role. Rusted buried steel gets replaced with polyethylene and tracer wire, and we use a listed transition riser where it rises to the house. Above-ground runs stay in steel. We test before backfilling so any problem joint is fixed while the trench is still open.
