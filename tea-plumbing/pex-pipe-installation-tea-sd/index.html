    Whole-Home PEX Repiping in Tea

    Low pressure at every faucet, across the whole house, usually points at the pipe and not the fixtures.     Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Tea, SD

    Think of a manifold like an electrical panel, but for water lines. Each fixture gets its own line from a central header, and each line gets its own valve, so you can shut off one toilet without killing water to the kitchen. You buy more pipe. The trade-off is almost no fittings hidden in the walls and steadier pressure when two showers run at once.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Tea, SD

    Gray polybutylene went into millions of homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorine in treated water attacks the plastic and the acetal fittings from the inside, so it can look fine from outside right up to the day a joint lets go. Many insurers ask whether you have it. Replacing it with PEX can make the house easier to insure and sell.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Tea

    Pinhole leaks in copper tend to come in clusters. Aggressive water, fast flow and soil contact under slabs can pit copper from the inside, and the first few pinholes are typically followed by more. Mineral scale builds up quickly in water heaters, faucet aerators and fixtures here, so a water softener is a common upgrade for homeowners. A switch to PEX ends it. Where new PEX meets existing copper stubs or valves, we use listed transition fittings.

    Leak Repair with PEX in Tea, SD

    A single bad section is not always a reason to repipe. We can cut out a split or corroded stretch of copper, transition to PEX with a listed fitting, and have the water back on in an hour or two. It is fine for an isolated leak. By the third leak, though, we will talk to you honestly about whether the rest of that copper is close behind.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Tea

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Both last decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.
