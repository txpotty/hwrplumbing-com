    Gas Meter to House Line Repair in Tea, SD

    The run from the utility meter to the house shutoff is the homeowner's pipe in most service areas, so the repair falls to a plumber and not the gas company. It is also the stretch that takes the most weather, sitting in the sun or the soil. Risers rust at grade. A new riser or a new buried run gets tested and left ready for the utility to turn on. Expect a half day for a typical riser swap.

    Underground Gas Line Repair in Tea

    Yard lines run from the meter to the house, and often on to a detached garage, a pool heater, a grill or an outdoor kitchen. Steel pipe in the ground corrodes wherever the wrap was nicked during the original install. Roots and settling do damage too. We call 811 for locates, dig at the suspected spot and replace the bad run with yellow polyethylene pipe and tracer wire. Sand goes around the new pipe before backfill, per Tea trench rules.

    Residential Gas Leak Detection in Tea

    A gas leak in a house rarely sits where the smell is strongest, because gas drifts along joists and collects in closets and basements. The first step is a pressure gauge on the piping, which tells us whether the system is actually losing gas. Then the sniffer comes out. A combustible gas detector follows the reading from fitting to fitting until it spikes. Bubble solution on the suspect joint gives the final answer.

    Propane Gas Leak Repair in Tea, SD

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. We check both regulators, the one at the tank and the one at the house. Low spots get sniffed first. If the supplier owns the tank, they handle the tank side and we handle the piping from there to the appliances. Rural properties with buried tanks get a line test too.

    Appliance Connector Replacement in Tea

    Old uncoated brass connectors, the kind made before the mid-1970s, are the subject of consumer safety warnings because the flare nut can separate from the tube. Any of those still in service should be replaced. We see them weekly. The replacement is a coated stainless connector sized for the appliance, with a new ball valve and a sediment trap where one is required. Bubble testing follows before the pilot is lit.
