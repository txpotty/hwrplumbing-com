    Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Tea

    Pricing depends on the job. Simple repairs like a faucet cartridge or toilet rebuild tend to cost $150 to $350, a water heater installation is often $1,200 to $3,500, and slab leak repairs or full repipes can reach several thousand dollars. The exact number comes after the diagnosis.

    A slab leak is a break in a water line running under a concrete foundation. Soils in South Dakota range from glacial deposits in the east to clay rich ground farther west that swells when wet and shrinks when dry. Warm spots on the floor, a sudden jump in the water bill or the sound of water running with every fixture off are the usual signs. It will not fix itself.

    Watch for drips that will not stop, slow drains, banging or whistling pipes, a sudden drop in pressure, discolored water and wet spots with no obvious source. Any one of these points to a problem behind the wall or under the floor. Do not ignore them.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Grease out of the drains and a yearly pressure test help too, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. An annual check finds small issues early.

    One to two hours covers many repairs. Plan on half a day for a water heater swap, and a whole-house repipe runs a few days, with the water turned back on each evening so the family can shower and cook. The schedule is set before we start.

    Every one to two years works for most homes in Tea, SD. The inspection usually runs about an hour, and we check pressure, look for slow leaks at shutoffs and supply lines, test the water heater relief valve and look for corrosion on exposed pipe. Small finds cost far less to fix.

    Keep grease out of the kitchen drain. Put strainers in the tub and shower and flush nothing but toilet paper, and find your main water shutoff now, before an emergency, so you are not hunting for it with water on the floor. Older homes benefit from a yearly check.

    Two leaks in a year is the tipping point for most homes. Polybutylene or galvanized supply pipe pushes it toward a repipe, too, since patching one spot on a failing system just moves the next leak a few feet down the line. We price both side by side.
