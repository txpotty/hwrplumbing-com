    What Sewer Repair Covers in Sioux Falls, SD

    Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Sioux Falls, SD

    Trenchless repair means we fix the pipe without opening a trench along its full length. Two access holes, sometimes one. Pipe bursting pulls a new HDPE line through the old one and splits the old pipe outward, while cured-in-place lining pushes a resin-soaked felt tube into the old pipe and hardens it into a new wall. Both save driveways and mature trees. We tell you which one your pipe can take.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Sioux Falls

    Some lines are too far gone for a liner. A fully collapsed section is one example. When the camera shows a collapse, a large offset or a belly deep enough to hold several inches of water, we open a trench, remove the old pipe and lay new Schedule 40 PVC on proper bedding at a quarter inch of fall per foot. Older homes often need this on at least one section. We stage the work so you lose sewer service for a few hours, not days.

    Sewer Pipe Relining in Sioux Falls, SD

    Relining builds a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line with a jetter first. A flexible liner saturated with epoxy is pulled or inverted into place, inflated against the old wall and left to cure, so every crack and leaking joint is covered by one jointless pipe. The finished pipe loses a little diameter. On a four-inch residential line, that rarely matters.

    Bellied Sewer Line Correction in Sioux Falls, SD

    A belly is a low spot where the pipe has sagged and holds standing water. Waste collects in the dip. Shifting clay and deep frost can strain buried sewer and water lines, so a camera inspection is useful when drains back up repeatedly. We measure the depth of standing water on camera, dig out that section, re-bed the pipe on compacted gravel and set it back to a quarter inch of fall per foot, which is the typical code minimum for a four-inch line. A shallow belly sometimes just needs regular cleaning.

    Video Camera Sewer Inspection in Sioux Falls

    A self-leveling camera goes in at the cleanout or through a pulled toilet. We record the whole run. Cracks, root intrusion, offset joints and standing water all show up on the screen, and the sonde in the camera head lets us mark the exact spot and depth on the ground above, which keeps the dig to one small pit. Older homes in Sioux Falls often hide two or three problems in one line, and the video catches all of them.

    Sewer Repair for Commercial Properties in Sioux Falls, SD

    Commercial laterals run six inches or larger and carry far more flow than a house. A closed kitchen loses revenue by the hour. For restaurants, strip centers and apartment buildings in Sioux Falls we schedule repairs after hours where we can, set up temporary bypass pumping when the line has to stay in service, and give managers a clear window to share with tenants. Grease trap outlet lines are one of our most common commercial repairs.

    Pipe Burst and Collapse Repair in Sioux Falls, SD

    A collapsed sewer pipe stops all flow at the point of failure. Every drain backs up. Collapses usually come from corroded cast iron, crushed clay under a driveway or tar-paper pipe that has finally flattened, and the camera tells us exactly where the pipe gave way and how deep it sits. The broken length comes out and new pipe goes in. Lining cannot fix a full collapse.

    Orangeburg and Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Sioux Falls

    Orangeburg is wood fiber soaked in coal tar. It flattens with age. Cast iron from the same era rusts from the inside, builds scale and eventually splits along the bottom where the sewage runs, so by the time a camera shows widespread damage, spot repairs are a poor investment. We replace these lines with PVC. Trenchless bursting is often an option.
