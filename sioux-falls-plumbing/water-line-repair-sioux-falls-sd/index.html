    Copper Water Line Repair in Sioux Falls

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Pinholes show up where stones press on the pipe, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. We usually cut out two feet. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, then we look over the pipe on either side for more pitting before we backfill.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Sioux Falls, SD

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Repeated leaks on galvanized pipe call for a new line. Age matters too, and a line past about 40 years is usually near the end no matter the material. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. We show you the pipe we exposed.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Sioux Falls, SD

    Replacement beats repair once a line has leaked two or three times, or when it is galvanized or polybutylene. Patching that kind of pipe tends to buy months, not years. We run new Type K copper or PEX from the meter to the house, usually in one day with a trench or a trenchless pull. We pull the permit, set up the inspection and pressure test the new line before it is covered.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Sioux Falls

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. With PEX, the fitting is the weak point most of the time. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Sioux Falls

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or a spot near the walk that stays muddy days after rain. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. The dig follows the sound.

    PEX Water Line Repair in Sioux Falls, SD

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rodents chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and sunlight exposure before burial weakens the plastic over time. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Sioux Falls, SD

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. We shut it down first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, and if the repair will run past a few hours we set a temporary bypass to the house. Same-day repairs are the norm. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Sioux Falls, SD

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. By the time one leaks, the rest of the pipe is close behind. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, which can hold for a while if the pipe is not too far gone. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. You get your flow back as a bonus.

You get a written estimate after we locate the leak.

    Age is the biggest cause. Old pipe corrodes. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, plus accidental hits from fence posts, irrigation trenching and utility work. Poor original installation is another, such as pipe laid on sharp rock.

    Labor is warrantied, always. New pipe and fittings carry the manufacturer warranty as well, and both terms are written on your estimate before any work starts. Keep the invoice. It is your proof of coverage.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. If it moves with everything off, you have a leak.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Resulting damage sometimes is. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, and that coverage usually pays for the line repair. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.
