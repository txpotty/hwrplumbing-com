    Main breaks, hydrant use and fire flow can drop city water pressure in seconds. When that happens, any cross-connection, a sprinkler line, a boiler or a hose in a bucket, can pull water back toward the main. The assembly is the barrier.

    Backflow preventers stop contaminated water from flowing backward into the public supply during pressure drops. Irrigation, fire lines and commercial equipment are the most common sources of that contamination, and water providers require assemblies on them to protect public health. It is the law in most places.

    Water dripping or running from the relief valve on an RPZ is the clearest sign. A pressure drop, a split bonnet after cold weather or water with an odd taste can also mean trouble. Have it checked promptly.

    An RPZ has two checks with a relief valve between them, and it is rated for high-hazard lines. A DCVA has two checks and no relief valve, so it is used for lower hazards like many fire lines and some irrigation systems. Code decides which one.
