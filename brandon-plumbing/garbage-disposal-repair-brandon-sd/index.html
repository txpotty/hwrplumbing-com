    Disposal Odor and Buildup Cleaning in Brandon, SD

    Food buildup and grease on the chamber walls and under the splash guard cause most disposal smells.     Electrical Problems and Resetting in Brandon

    No hum at all usually means no power reaching the motor. The red reset button on the bottom is the first stop, then check the breaker, the wall switch and the outlet or junction box under the sink with a meter. Cabinets are damp places. In that damp cabinet, wire nuts corrode and cord connections loosen, so we replace them instead of retightening.

    Slow Draining Disposal Repair in Brandon, SD

    Good grinding but slow draining usually means the discharge tube or the trap arm is packed. Coffee grounds and rice cause most of it. The trap comes off, the tube and arm get cleared with a properly sized cable, and we confirm the arm drops about a quarter inch per foot.

    Flange and Inlet Leak Repair in Brandon, SD

    The flange, dishwasher inlet, discharge tube and body are where leaks start. We trace the leak, reseal the flange with plumber's putty and replace gaskets or tubes as needed. Water from the bottom usually means a failed internal seal, which makes replacement the smarter move. It is not a repairable part.

    Noisy Disposal Repair in Brandon

    Grinding, rattling or a hum that sounds rough all point to something inside. Loose mounting rings rattle too. We check the mount first, then the chamber for debris, then listen to the motor with the chamber empty, because a motor that whines with nothing in it has bad bearings.

    Jammed Disposal Repair in Brandon

    A jam usually comes from one hard object, like a peach pit, a chicken bone or a twist tie wrapped around the impeller. We free the flywheel from below with the hex key and pull the object with pliers, never a hand, then run it with water to make sure nothing else is stuck. We press the reset after clearing it. Check the trap arm if it jams again.

    InSinkErator makes the Badger line and the Evolution series, and we repair both. Badgers are simple single-stage units, while Evolution models have multi-stage grinding and, on some models, a separate stage that can jam on its own. Common parts are on board. Motors on these units are not sold separately.
