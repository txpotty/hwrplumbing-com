    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Brandon

    Propane homes in Brandon can run tankless units too, using a model built or converted for propane. Propane runs at a higher pressure than natural gas, so the regulator, gas line sizing and the unit's gas valve all have to match, and the propane supplier may need to change the regulator at the tank. A small tank can run short.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Brandon

    Gas tankless units in Brandon are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. The catch is gas supply. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Brandon, SD

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Brandon, SD homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. Whole-house electric units pull a lot of current. Some models need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, more than a 100-amp panel can usually spare alongside the range and AC. We check the panel first.

    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Brandon

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirc pump on a timer or sensor keeps the hot line primed, cutting the wait at the far sink from about a minute to a few seconds. It saves water too.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Brandon

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and several models come with a recirculation pump built in. The built-in pump can move hot water toward a distant bath on a timer, cutting the wait at the tap without a separate pump. Venting is usually two-inch or three-inch PVC. Condensing units also need a condensate drain.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Brandon, SD

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A typical 40-gallon gas tank uses around 40,000 BTU, while a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the gas line from the meter almost always gets upsized. Venting changes too. We plan both on the quote.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Brandon, SD

    Replacing an old tankless unit is usually simpler than a first-time conversion. Since the gas line and vent are already there, most swaps mean matching connections, inspecting the old vent and mounting the new heater. Different brands vent differently. We confirm fit first.

    Tankless vs Tank Cost Comparison in Brandon, SD

    Upfront, a tankless install in Brandon, SD costs more than a standard tank because of the unit price, the gas line upgrade and the venting changes. The gap shrinks over time. Lower standby loss, a lifespan of up to 20 years against about 10 for a tank, and parts that can be replaced instead of the whole unit all count toward the total cost of ownership. Ask for both prices.
