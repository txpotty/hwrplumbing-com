    Agricultural and Ranch Well Pumps in Spearfish

    Irrigation and stock wells often run 3 to 10 HP pumps, sometimes on three-phase power with a motor starter and overload protection at the panel. Starters and overloads get replaced along with the pump. We schedule around watering times.

    Emergency Well Pump Replacement in Spearfish

    Dry faucets on a well-only property mean no flushing or cooking, so we treat those as same-day calls whenever the crew and the weather allow. The truck carries common 1/2 and 3/4 HP pumps. Splice kits and pressure switches ride along too.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Spearfish, SD

    No-water calls often end at the control box, not the pump. Three-wire pumps have a control box with a start capacitor and relay, and those parts fail far more often than the motor itself. We test them first. Swapping a capacitor costs a fraction of pulling a pump.

    Grundfos Well Pump Replacement in Spearfish, SD

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have a built-in soft start and dry-run protection, and they run on two wires with no control box at the surface. They are pricier than a standard pump. Add the CU 301 controller to an SQE and you get constant pressure.

    Well Pump Sizing in Spearfish

    Sizing starts with the well log. The log, or a sounder down the casing, gives us static water level and well depth, and a draw-down test tells us roughly how many gallons per minute the well can actually give. Pump more than the well yields and it runs dry.

    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Spearfish

    Sand kills submersibles early. A well that pumps sand will wear impellers down in a few years, so we check for grit in the old pump and talk about setting the new one higher off the bottom or adding a sand separator. Pump setting depth matters.

    Constant Pressure Well Pump Installation in Spearfish, SD

    Constant pressure systems hold the house at one pressure, say 50 psi, whether one faucet is open or the shower and the irrigation are running together. A variable frequency drive speeds the pump up and slows it down to match demand. Showers stop going weak.
