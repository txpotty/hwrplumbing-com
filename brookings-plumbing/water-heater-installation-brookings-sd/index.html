    Electric Water Heater Installation in Brookings, SD

    Electric tanks need a 240-volt circuit, usually a 30-amp double-pole breaker with 10-gauge wire. Before hooking up the new tank we check that the breaker and wire size match the elements, since a mismatch is a common cause of tripped breakers and scorched terminals. They do not need a vent. That makes placement easier.

    Major Brand Water Heater Installation in Brookings, SD

    Rheem, Bradford White, A.O. Smith and Noritz are the brands we install most. Each has its own install manual, and following it to the letter is what keeps the tank warranty valid if something fails in year six. Our crews know each one. You can compare warranty length and recovery rate with us before you buy.

    Attic and Closet Water Heater Installs in Brookings

    Attic and closet installs take more planning than a garage swap. An attic heater needs a pan with its own drain line, a properly routed relief line and enough access to get the old tank out and the new one in. Ceilings are at stake. If moving it makes sense, we can relocate the heater.

    Hybrid Heat Pump Water Heater Installation in Brookings, SD

    Heat pump water heaters pull warmth from the surrounding air and can use about a third of the power of a standard electric tank. A hybrid needs open air space, often around 700 cubic feet, plus a condensate line to a drain, which rules out most small closets. Some qualify for tax credits. We help you decide if one fits.

    Commercial Water Heater Installation in Brookings

    Restaurants, salons and offices burn through hot water at rates no household tank can keep up with. We install commercial tanks over 100,000 BTU and racked tankless systems, with the venting and gas supply those loads require. We schedule around your hours. Installs can happen after close.

    Expansion Tank Installation in Brookings, SD

    A pressure reducing valve or check valve on the main turns your plumbing into a closed system. When the water heats and expands with nowhere to go, pressure climbs and stresses the relief valve, the toilet fill valves and every supply line in the house. An expansion tank absorbs it. We pre-charge it to match your water pressure.

    Traditional Tank Water Heater Installation in Brookings

    Tank heaters still heat most homes' water, holding 40 to 75 gallons at temperature until you need it. Sizing is based on the first-hour rating on the yellow label, which tells you how much hot water the tank can deliver during a busy hour. Gas or electric both work. We connect the fuel, set the vent and haul the old tank away.

    Gas Tank Water Heater Installation in Brookings

    A gas tank is the most common water heater we install, in 40, 50 and 75 gallon sizes. Each one gets a new gas connector, a sediment trap and a shutoff valve, plus a vent connector pitched up toward the flue at a quarter inch per foot. Then a draft test. Gas suits busy households.
