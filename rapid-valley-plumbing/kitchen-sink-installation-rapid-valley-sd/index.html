    Granite and Quartz Composite Sinks in Rapid Valley

    Composite sinks are heavier than stainless and usually thicker at the drain, so standard strainers sometimes need a longer threaded body. We check the strainer fit first.     Undermount Kitchen Sink Installation in Rapid Valley

    Undermount sinks rely on clips and adhesive to stay attached to granite or quartz. The bowl is mounted, the rim sealed with silicone and the sink braced while the adhesive sets, normally overnight before it can take a full bowl of water. Then come the strainers and trap.

    Emergency Sink Replacement in Rapid Valley

    A cracked bowl or a rusted-through strainer can flood a cabinet within hours. First, close both angle stops and stop using the sink. Common stainless sizes ride on the truck. If you have a standard 33 by 22 inch drop-in, we can usually remove the damaged sink and set a new one that day.

    Drop-In Kitchen Sink Installation in Rapid Valley

    Sitting on the counter by their rim, drop-in sinks are a natural fit for laminate, butcher block and tile. It is often the quickest swap. After checking the cutout, we seal the rim and draw the clips down evenly, and in older homes and rentals it is often a two-hour job.

    Apron-Front Sinks in Rapid Valley, SD

    Apron sinks need a cabinet cut to fit the front panel, and the bowl must be set before the counter gets measured.     Composite Sink Installation in Rapid Valley

    Granite and quartz composite sinks resist scratches on the surface but can chip at the drain hole if a strainer is overtightened. We tighten by hand first.     Stainless Steel Sink Installation in Rapid Valley

    Stainless sinks come in gauges from 16 to 22, and the lower the number, the thicker the steel.
