    Dishwasher Connection With Disposal Installation in Rapid Valley

    Every new disposal has a plug sealing the dishwasher inlet. We knock it out with a screwdriver and hammer, fish the plug out of the chamber and connect the hose with a new clamp.     Disposals in Remodels and New Builds in Rapid Valley

    On new builds and remodels in Rapid Valley, we work with your contractor so the disposal, the sink and the dishwasher are planned together.     High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in Rapid Valley

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit is bigger and heavier than a starter model, so we measure the cabinet first.     Switching From Batch-Feed to Continuous-Feed in Rapid Valley, SD

    A continuous-feed unit needs a switch, which a batch-feed kitchen may not have. That can be a wall switch, an air switch button on the sink deck or a switched outlet under the sink, and the choice depends on how hard it is to run wire to the spot you want. An air button skips the wall wiring. The drain usually stays the same.

    Standard Disposal Installation in Rapid Valley

    Most households in Rapid Valley do well with a 1/3 to 1/2 horsepower continuous-feed unit. Wiring and testing come last. A smooth spin and a clean drain tell us it is right.

    Air Switch Disposal Installation in Rapid Valley, SD

    An air button on the sink deck or counter turns the disposal on. The button pushes air through a tube to a control box in the cabinet, which keeps electricity away from the button and avoids a new wall switch. They are common on islands.

    Disposal Installation on an Existing Sink in Rapid Valley

    For a common 1/2 or 3/4 HP disposal, we remove the old unit if there is one and install the new one on the existing sink. Tight cabinets need planning. Sometimes a compact model is the better fit.
