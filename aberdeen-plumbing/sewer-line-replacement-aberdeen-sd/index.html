    Sewer Line Replacement Options in Aberdeen, SD

    Trenchless Sewer Replacement in Aberdeen, SD

    Two holes instead of one long trench. Pipe bursting drags a cone-shaped head through the old line, cracking it outward while new fused HDPE follows right behind. Lining is the other trenchless route. A resin-soaked tube is pulled into the cleaned host pipe and cured in place, which leaves a new pipe inside the old one and works well when the original still holds its round shape.

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Replacement in Aberdeen, SD

    Orangeburg is tar-soaked wood fiber. It was installed in a lot of homes in the decades after the war, and over time it softens, blisters and squashes into an oval that catches everything going by. Clay holds up better, except at the joints. Several bad spots on one run usually make full replacement the better buy.

    Cured-in-Place Sewer Lining in Aberdeen

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line first, often with a cutter and a jetter, then pull a resin-saturated felt tube into place and inflate it so it cures hard against the host pipe. Collapsed pipe will not work. It is a strong choice under slabs and finished rooms. You will get a straight answer on whether yours is a candidate.

    Commercial Sewer Lateral Work in Aberdeen, SD

    Apartment buildings, offices and restaurants in Aberdeen, SD need lateral work too. Bigger flow and heavier grease mean careful sizing, extra cleanouts for jetting and attention to the grease interceptor tie-in. Some jobs need traffic control. We schedule around tenants and posted hours.

    How We Price Sewer Replacement in Aberdeen

    Length and depth drive the price. A forty-foot lateral four feet down in an open lawn costs a lot less than ninety feet under a driveway at eight feet, and we price both honestly. Method matters too. Your written estimate lists labor, pipe, equipment, permit fees and restoration as separate lines. The total is what you pay.

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Aberdeen

    Cast iron rots from the inside out. The bottom of a cast iron pipe goes first, and the camera will show a trench rusted through even when the top looks fine. Scale snags paper and grease. We swap the outdoor section for plastic pipe. For cast iron under a slab, we talk through lining versus rerouting.

    How Pipe Bursting Works in Aberdeen

    A winch pulls the bursting head. Because the head is a little wider than the old pipe, it cracks it into the surrounding dirt as the new HDPE follows. Only two pits get dug. With no joints in the new pipe, roots have no way back in.

    Sewer Pipe Materials We Install in Aberdeen, SD

    PVC covers most of our trenched work. SDR 35 is the common sewer grade for buried laterals, Schedule 40 shows up where code or the inspector wants a heavier wall, and HDPE is our pick for bursting. Soils in South Dakota range from glacial deposits in the east to clay rich ground farther west that swells when wet and shrinks when dry. Cast iron still gets used indoors on some jobs. The material choice depends on depth, soil and the method you pick.
