    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Aberdeen

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. Most of the difference is labor, mainly digging and concrete repair. Permits add a little. We quote in writing after a site visit, with the trap, the labor and the permit fees listed as separate lines.

    Two warranties apply here. Manufacturer coverage on the unit varies by brand and material, and plastic bodies usually get the longer terms. We cover our workmanship, meaning leaks at our joints and settling of a tank we set, and we give you the terms in writing before we start.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. You get copies of every approval for your files. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. We register the unit with the maker so the factory warranty is on record. Keep your cleaning logs. Some manufacturers ask for proof of regular service before they honor a claim on a cracked body or a failed baffle.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. The trade usually calls the small indoor unit a trap and the big outdoor tank an interceptor. Codes vary on the wording. Some plumbing codes call both of them interceptors and separate them as hydromechanical and gravity, so we use whatever terms your permit office uses.

    It keeps cooking grease out of the public sewer. Hot greasy water cools as it moves through the trap, the grease floats to the top, and cleaner water leaves through the outlet. Without one, that grease hardens in the city mains and causes sewer overflows, and sewer authorities fine the kitchens they trace it to. Your own drain lines benefit too.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. Busy kitchens with small indoor traps often hit that mark every week. Outdoor tanks go longer. Many gravity interceptors are pumped every 30 to 90 days, and your local FOG program may set a maximum interval no matter what the trap looks like.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. Schier's plastic bodies do not rust, which helps in hot, wet kitchens. Brand matters less than sizing. A well-known trap installed too small will still fail inspection, so we pick the size first and the brand second.
