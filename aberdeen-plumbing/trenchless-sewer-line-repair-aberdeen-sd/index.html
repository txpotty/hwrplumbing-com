    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Aberdeen, SD

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. The crew saturates a felt tube with two-part epoxy on a roller table at the truck, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Cure time runs a few hours. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.

    Commercial Trenchless Sewer Lining in Aberdeen, SD

    Office and retail buildings often have a cast iron building drain under the slab that has rotted along the bottom. Lining it avoids cutting the floor.     Causes of Sewer Line Failure in Aberdeen, SD

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints separate, crack or drop out of grade over the years. Soils in South Dakota range from glacial deposits in the east to clay rich ground farther west that swells when wet and shrinks when dry. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Age does the rest. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under the weight of soil and traffic.

    Perma-Liner Trenchless Solutions in Aberdeen

    Perma-Liner makes inversion drums and epoxy systems for lining 2- to 6-inch drain lines, which covers most of what we see in houses. The setup is compact enough to fit in a pickup bed and roll through a side gate. Compact gear matters on tight lots. The resin is mixed on site, so it does not start curing before the liner is in place.

    Root Intrusion Repair in Aberdeen, SD

    Roots enter at the joints, where clay bells or cast iron hubs have a small gap or a failed gasket. Once in, they fill the pipe. We cut them out with a mechanical root cutter or a hydro jet, then line the pipe so those joints are sealed for good and the line stops needing a cleaning every few months. With no joints left, there is nowhere for them to return.

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacements in Aberdeen

    A bursting head is a steel cone, slightly wider than the old pipe, pulled through the line by a hydraulic unit or a winch cable. It cracks the old pipe outward into the soil while a string of new HDPE pipe follows right behind it. Fused joints, no gaps. This is the fix for collapsed pipe.
