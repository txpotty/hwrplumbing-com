    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Installation in Pierre

    Residential water lines usually run $1,800 to $5,500 depending on length, soil and material. Long runs through rock, trenchless boring or copper pipe can push it to $6,000 to $10,000.

    Most jurisdictions require a permit and an inspection for a new or replaced service line. The permit and the inspection are part of our job.

    This is a job for a licensed plumber. You are dealing with excavation, pipe sizing and code rules, plus coordination with the water utility at the meter, and a poor install can lead to leaks, water damage and contamination of your drinking water. The risk is not worth it.

    Our water line installs carry a warranty on parts and labor. The specific terms come with your written estimate, and the pipe manufacturer adds its own long coverage, which on PEX and HDPE often runs decades when the pipe is installed to spec. Your records stay on file with us.

    For PEX we usually install Uponor, Viega or Zurn systems with fittings from the same maker.     Type K copper, PEX and HDPE are all approved for buried service. The plastic options flex and need fewer joints, while copper gives you rigidity and puncture resistance.

    Manufacturers back PEX and HDPE with long warranties, and copper has decades of history. We also warranty our labor, and we go over the terms with you before work starts.

    PEX is flexible, cheaper, and has fewer fittings, so there are fewer places for a leak to start. Copper is rigid, costs more and has a long track record, and it handles heat and pressure well, so the right choice depends on your budget and the water chemistry in Pierre. Either one meets code.
