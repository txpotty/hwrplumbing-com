    Grundfos Well Pump Replacement in Pierre, SD

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have a built-in soft start and dry-run protection, and they run on two wires with no control box at the surface. They cost more up front. The SQE pairs with a CU 301 controller for constant pressure.

    Jet Pump Replacement in Pierre, SD

    Jet pumps sit above ground, in a well house or basement, and pull water up by suction from wells where the water level is within about 25 feet. Deep-well jets with a two-pipe setup can reach further, but they work harder. The common failure is losing prime. Usually a leaking foot valve or a cracked suction line lets the water fall back down the well, so we check both before we blame the pump.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Pierre

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes a healthy pump look sick. If the pump clicks on every time someone runs a faucet for ten seconds, the bladder in the tank has probably failed and the pump is short cycling itself to death. We check the air charge with a gauge. Precharge should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure.

    Pulling a Submersible Pump in Pierre

    Pulling a submersible means lifting the pump and every length of drop pipe out through the pitless adapter, sometimes hundreds of feet of it. We check the torque arrestor and every splice on the way up. Most jobs are done in a day.

    Emergency Well Pump Replacement in Pierre

    Dry faucets on a well-only property mean no flushing or cooking, so we treat those as same-day calls whenever the crew and the weather allow. The truck carries common 1/2 and 3/4 HP pumps. We also bring splice kits and switches.

    No-Water Calls in Pierre

    Pump trouble rarely gives notice. One morning the faucet coughs air and then nothing, and by then the motor has often been struggling for weeks. We test the control box and pressure switch before pulling anything.

    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Pierre, SD

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. A pinhole in the suction pipe lets air in, the pump loses prime, and it runs dry until the seal burns up. The suction line gets a pressure test. If the well is too deep for a jet pump, we will say so and quote a submersible.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Pierre

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Most fail from sand chewing up the impellers or a lightning surge that cooks the windings. Before pulling, we test the windings with a megohmmeter. That tells us whether the problem is down the hole or up top.
