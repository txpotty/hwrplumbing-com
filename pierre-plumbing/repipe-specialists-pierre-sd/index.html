    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Pierre, SD

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. The pipe gets brittle from the inside, and fittings crack, so a home that has had one polybutylene leak is usually at risk everywhere else in the system. Full replacement is the fix. Many insurers ask about it.

    Condo, Townhome and Multi-Family Repipes in Pierre

    Shared walls and HOA rules make repiping attached homes more involved. We coordinate with the HOA, schedule water shutoffs that may affect neighbors and protect adjoining units from dust and noise as much as we can. Building-wide jobs go in phases. Big buildings get repiped by stack or wing, which lets residents stay home.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Pierre, SD

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, and over decades the inside diameter closes down to a fraction of what it was. Pressure drops slowly. Rust-colored water in the morning, poor pressure upstairs and dripping threaded joints all point to failing galvanized. We replace it with PEX or copper. Most homeowners notice the pressure difference the first time they turn on the shower.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Pierre

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Many homes built from the 1980s through the early 2000s have one or the other. We can usually identify it from the pipe at the water heater, and full replacement makes the house easier to insure and sell.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Pierre

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. Rerouting one branch overhead and leaving the bad pipe in the slab is often cheaper than both a repipe and a jackhammer repair. This works well for a first leak. After multiple leaks, we will show you why a full repipe may cost less over the next ten years.

    Whole-Home Copper Repiping in Pierre

It holds up well, does not mind sunlight in an exposed run and has a long track record behind it. It costs more.     Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Pierre

    PEX repipes in Pierre commonly run $4,000 to $15,000. Copper costs more. Copper repipes often fall between $8,000 and $25,000 or more, and the biggest factors are the size of the house, the number of bathrooms and fixtures and how easy the attic or crawlspace is to work in. We give you an itemized price after the walk-through.

    PEX resists scale and pinhole corrosion and handles expansion well, which makes it a strong choice for hard water. Copper is durable but costs more and can pinhole in aggressive water.

    Mineral scale builds up quickly in water heaters, faucet aerators and fixtures here, so a water softener is a common upgrade for homeowners. Hard water deposits calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe over time. Flow drops as the bore narrows. Scale also speeds corrosion in metal lines, which is why PEX-A plus a softener or filter added during a repipe can protect the new system and your fixtures.

    We have to open some drywall, but the openings are small and planned. Most are about the size of a sheet of paper. We cut clean squares at fixtures and along the route, patch every one after the inspection and match the existing texture, so the walls are ready to paint when we finish.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. Buyers notice it. Swapping out polybutylene, Kitec or galvanized makes a home easier to insure and sell, especially with a permit record and transferable warranty.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A with ProPEX fittings and Type L copper from domestic mills. Copper jobs often get Viega ProPress fittings.

    Both work well, and the best choice depends on your house and your water. PEX costs less. PEX is flexible, handles a freeze better and resists scale, while copper is rigid, tolerates sunlight and has a long history, but it costs more and can pinhole in aggressive water.

    We provide a written workmanship warranty, and PEX makers such as Uponor offer long-term warranties when certified installers use their system. You get the details before we start.
